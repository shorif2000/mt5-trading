# XAUUSD Version 1.0 and 1.1: why the strategy loses money

**Date:** 2026-11-10. **Scope:** diagnosis of the committed backtests only. No strategy rule,
parameter, risk setting or report was changed, and MT5 was not accessed.

- **Runs reviewed:** Version 1.0 `20261110T222100Z_7f525aace4` and Version 1.1
  `20261110T223948Z_9caf03ac73`, both in the base cost scenario (entry 7 points, assumed; stop
  exits p90 = 40 points, from measured fills).
- **Evidence tables:** `xauusd_v1.0_v1.1_diagnostic.md`, generated by
  `scripts/xauusd_diagnostics.py` from the run manifests and the same history file (checked by
  sha256).
- **Reconciliation:** trades, net, profit factor, win rate, maximum consecutive losses and mean
  holding time match the committed reports exactly for both versions and all periods.
- **2026 is development evidence, not out-of-sample.** It was examined while Version 1.1 was
  designed.
- **Units:** results are in R (net ÷ estimated loss at sizing). The paper ledger compounds, so
  GBP totals mostly track the balance path.

## Summary

1. **There is no measurable edge before costs.** Gross expectancy (before spread and slippage)
   is between −0.02R and +0.07R per trade in every period and both versions. Gross profit factor
   is 0.94–1.13.
2. **Costs turn that near-zero edge into losses.** Spread and slippage cost 0.03–0.13R per
   trade. Net expectancy is −0.115R per trade (Version 1.0, Training), and Training is the only
   period whose confidence interval excludes zero.
3. **Costs are a fixed number of points, so tight stops are hit hardest.** Stops under $3 per
   ounce lose 0.18R per trade to costs; stops of $12–25 lose 0.03R. 2023–24 gold had small ATRs
   (median stop $3.29), so Training bears most of the cost damage. That is the main reason
   Training loses and 2025–26 is close to break-even.
4. **Exits cap the payoff below the 2R design.** About 25% of trades reach the target, at
   +1.6R to +1.9R after costs. About 16–21% are force-closed at 16:45 New York (average +0.2R)
   and 2–4% exit on H1 trend invalidation (average −0.54R). The realised win/loss ratio is
   1.5–1.8, so break-even needs a win rate of about 36–39%. The actual win rate is 33–40%.
5. **The Version 1.1 filter does not select better trades.** On the same Version 1.0 trade list,
   the trades the filter rejects did *better* than the ones it keeps in Training (−0.064R
   against −0.137R) and in Validation (+0.078R against +0.001R). Only in 2026 is it the other way
   round, and that period was used to design the filter.
6. **No session, regime or entry-location bucket is consistently positive across periods.**
   London (03:00–08:00 New York) is the most consistently negative session, but it is positive in
   2025. Every bucket result is compatible with zero edge once sampling error is allowed for.

### Exit structure

| Exit (Version 1.0, all periods) | Trades | Mean R | Sum R |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop | 913 | −0.96 | −878 |

- Targets realise +1.73R, not +2R. Costs account for part of the gap, and the target is set 2R
  from the quoted entry rather than the filled price.
- Forced closes make a small positive contribution. That says nothing about whether holding
  overnight would help, which this data cannot answer.

### Account size

The £1,000 ledger falls to about £200 in Training. Below that level, many signals fail the 2%
rule at the 0.01-lot minimum:

| Version | Training signals rejected for minimum volume | Training trades at the 0.01-lot minimum |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 774 | 406 of 823 |
| 1.1 | 428 | 242 of 636 |

As the balance shrinks, the only trades that pass are those with tight stops, which are the
most cost-heavy. So the ledger mechanics may deepen the Training loss. The R figures above are
not affected by this, but the GBP totals and drawdowns are.

## 4. Statistical reliability

- **Sample size.** Validation and 2026 cannot be distinguished from zero edge. Their 95%
  intervals are about ±0.12R wide: Version 1.0 Validation is −0.084R to +0.133R. Only Training
  is clearly negative (−0.186R to −0.040R).
- **Few trades decide the sign.** Removing the best 10 trades turns Version 1.0 Validation from
  +9.7R to −9.7R.
- **Repeated signals.** About 26–28% of trades share a pullback with another trade (Version 1.0
  Training: 823 trades from 701 distinct pullbacks), so the effective sample is smaller than the
  trade count. The bootstrap treats trades as independent, so the intervals are, if anything,
  too narrow.
- **Concentration.** 17 of 24 Training months lose. The worst months are July 2023, February
  2023 and August 2025, each losing −14R to −16R. The losses are spread out, not one event.
- **Selection bias.**
  - Version 1.1 was specified after 2026 had been seen, and its 2026 result is the only one in
    its favour.
  - This review compared many buckets, so a few will look good by chance. Any bucket that looks
    good here (the overlap session, "mixed" efficiency, "normal" volatility in 2025) is a
    hypothesis, not a finding.

## 5. Where evidence is insufficient

- **Entry slippage is unknown.** It is a scenario, and every result moves with it.
- **Intrabar spread** (news spikes, rollover) is not modelled. There is one spread value per M15
  bar.
- **Stop slippage** is applied as a fixed percentile, not a distribution conditioned on
  volatility or time of day.
- **Historical margin** uses today's leverage and conversion rate. Tick values use historical
  GBPUSD.
- **No news or economic calendar.** Losses around scheduled releases cannot be separated out.
- **Bucket samples are too small.** Most hold 50–275 trades, too few to establish any regime or
  session effect.

## 6. Recommendations for further research

These are for research only, not rule changes. Each would need a pre-registered specification
and a test on data not yet seen (after 2026-10-30).

1. **Quantify the cost hurdle before anything else.** Report each candidate setup's expected
   cost in R at entry (spread plus slippage ÷ stop distance). Measure how much gross edge the
   strategy would need at today's volatility. Any future version should first show a gross
   profit factor clearly above 1. None of the current variants does.
2. **Measure entry slippage properly.** Record the requested and filled price in a demo
   environment, or use proxy measurements, to replace the 7-point assumption.
3. **Test the range-bound hypothesis prospectively.** Fix the definition now and observe it on
   new data. Don't tune it on 2023–2026.
4. **Separate account-size effects from strategy effects.** Re-run with a fixed-risk
   (non-compounding) ledger. Rejections at minimum volume and 0.01-lot rounding then wouldn't
   change which trades are taken.
5. **Count pullback events, not candles.** Report results per distinct pullback so that repeated
   signals don't inflate the sample.
6. **Reconsider whether the Version 1.1 real-yield filter is worth pursuing.** On the evidence
   here it removes better-than-average trades in two of three periods.

No condition identified here is shown to produce profitable trades.
